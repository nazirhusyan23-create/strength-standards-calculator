Why bodyweight-ratio strength standards?

Comparing lifters by the raw weight on the bar hides the real picture — a 140 lb woman deadlifting 245 lb and a 220 lb man deadlifting 385 lb are both pulling roughly 1.75x bodyweight, and both are equally strong relative to their frame. Every calculator in this collection converts your set into a bodyweight-ratio (or, for holds and calisthenics reps, a rep/time standard) so you can see where you actually stand — Beginner, Novice, Intermediate, Advanced, or Elite — instead of just a number on a plate.

Six calculators cover barbell lifts (bench press, squat, deadlift, overhead press) using the Epley 1RM formula from a recent working set. Nine cover calisthenics and gymnastic strength — pull-ups, dips, push-ups, muscle-ups, pistol squats, handstand push-ups, and the L-sit — using reps or hold time directly, since bodyweight movements don't always need an external load to be meaningful.
